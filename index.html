How we got to this exact formulation

Eight specialists.
One computational backbone.

Getting eight actives to cooperate at trial-grade dose is not a clinical question — it's a combinatorial one. Too many variables for ten clinicians to brute-force. So we did what AI-driven biotechs do: we used the tools.

Every dose, ratio, and interaction in glo was simulated against published trial data — then reviewed, adjusted, and signed off by the MDs. The computer narrowed the search space. The clinicians made the calls.

800Kdata points
01 · The dataset
Clinical trial data, indexed.
Published dose-response curves, bioavailability studies, and interaction data across the eight active ingredient families — pulled from peer-reviewed literature and structured for computational search.
3Bsimulations
02 · The search
Three billion combinations, modeled.
Dose, ratio, MW fraction, absorption window, drug-drug interaction matrix. Three billion configurations simulated to surface the small set worth clinical review.
8MDs · 4 yrs
03 · The judgment
Then clinicians chose.
The simulation surfaced candidates. The 8-specialist collective — 6 MDs (Korean clinicians + 1 US dermatologist) and 2 PhDs (skin reverse-aging research, Seoul + US) — reviewed, adjusted, ran patient-side validation, and signed off. AI is a fast literature search, not a doctor.

This is what AI in longevity actually means. Not "AI-generated supplements." Not a chatbot writing dosages. A computational tool that lets ten clinicians do four years of dose-calibration work in months — and lets us prove, with receipts, that every milligram on the label sits in the published evidence range.
